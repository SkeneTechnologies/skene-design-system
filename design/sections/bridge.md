## Use for

A whole cream band (a `<section>`, not a card) that lays N `BridgeNode` cards in a row, interleaves arrows between them, and inverts one of them to dark and lifts it. It exists to argue POSITION — that the featured thing sits between the others — and the tone difference is the argument, not styling: three cards in one tone read as three peers and the band says nothing. Structurally it is also the package's one doubly-nested inversion (`light` band, `dark` card inside it), so it doubles as the worked example of how the two subtree switches nest.

## Watch for

| note | via |
| --- | --- |
| The `light` class on the root is unconditional and written first, and the featured card's `dark` is the counterpart. `cn` is tailwind-merge, so a `className` background WILL win over `bg-brand-light` while leaving `light` in place — override the fill with anything dark and you get exactly the invisible-text failure (`text.primary` #faf1e9 on #faf1e9) the file header documents. | `prop BridgeNode.featured` |
| Arrows are unconditional between children — there is no prop to suppress them. Anything you pass as a child that is not part of a left-to-right sequence still gets an arrow after it unless it is last. | `prop Bridge.children` |
| `featured` is a per-node boolean with nothing enforcing exactly one. Two featured cards render two dark lifted cards and the band loses its point. Also note the lift is `md:-translate-y-3`: below `md` the row stacks and the featured card is dark but not raised. | `prop BridgeNode.featured` |
| The kicker is a hand-rolled Eyebrow-SHAPED chip, deliberately not `<Eyebrow>` — `Eyebrow` is built from `chrome.surface.border` / `chrome.text.muted`, which are invariant and render near-invisible on cream. The same trap applies to anything you pass into any slot that uses `chrome.text.*`. | `prop Bridge.eyebrow` |
| The outer cards' fill is `bg-surface-0` (mode-aware → #fafafa inside the band), deliberately a shade off the band's cream `brand.light`. Do not "fix" it to `bg-brand-light` — the cards then disappear into the band. | `prop BridgeNode.featured` |

## Constraints

From `machine/components.yaml` (`sections.Bridge`).

| prop | allowed |
| --- | --- |
| `featured` | boolean on BridgeNode — the dark, raised middle card |
| `items` | short fragments under the rule; three is the shape |
| `icon` | glyph above the label; on the live section only the featured card has one |

- Doubly nested inversion, and both halves are load-bearing. The band root carries `light` over brand.light; the featured card carries `dark` back over chrome.surface.1. Drop either class and every mode-aware token in that subtree keeps the wrong value against the fill.
- Nothing rendered here uses chrome.text.*. The band flips, so all type is the theme-aware text.* role. The two hairlines are the exception: chrome.line.onLight on the cream cards, chrome.line.subtle on the dark one, because no mode-aware role covers that pair.
- The kicker is an Eyebrow-SHAPED chip, not `<Eyebrow>`. Eyebrow is built from chrome.surface.border and chrome.text.muted and renders near-invisible on cream. `<Accent>` inside `title` does compose — brand peach is mode-aware and lands on its light value inside the band.
- Bridge owns the arrows and interleaves them between its children, so N nodes get N-1. A node that drew its own trailing arrow would have to know it is not last. Below md the row stacks and each arrow rotates a quarter turn rather than being dropped.
- The featured card's FILL stays invariant chrome.surface.* on purpose: that card is dark in every context. The `dark` class is for the type inside it, not for the fill.

## What binds this module

- **Polarity `applies-both`.** It has both forms and applies one of them; which is a call-site decision. Read Constraints above before placing it on a ground that flips.
- **`chrome.*` is invariant.** This module is in the fixed-dark world, so the distinction bites here: `chrome.*` never inverts and belongs only on a surface that never flips. Anything on a surface that follows the theme uses `text.*` / `surface.*`. The two share their dark values, so the wrong pick looks correct until someone opens light mode.
- **Content is props.** This section hardcodes no copy; the page supplies it.

These are the rules this module can break. The full set, the surface roles and
the contrast floors are in [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md).
