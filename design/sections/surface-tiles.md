## Constraints

From `machine/components.yaml` (`sections.SurfaceTiles`).

| prop | allowed |
| --- | --- |
| `selected` | the cream tile — applies `light` to that tile |
| `accent` | `peach`, `violet`, `blue`, `neutral` |

- Not OverviewTiles. That one is caption/number/note and exists so four counts read as commensurable; these are icon/name/note with exactly one selected. A metric tile cannot be "chosen".
- selected carries the `light` class, which is the whole mechanism. On a cream tile without it, text.primary is #faf1e9 on #faf1e9. The unselected tiles paint their own near-black ground and so take the invariant chrome.* roles legitimately.
- Selection is presentational: no onSelect, no internal state. The band that uses this is editorial, and a caller who wants a real picker owns the state and passes the flag.
- SurfaceDetail is a SIBLING of the row, not a child. Nesting it would force the row to own selection state to know where to render it.
- These render on a SectionBackdrop field, and SurfaceDetail is deliberately translucent so the halftone reads through it. A solid fill punches a rectangle out of the field.

## What binds this module

- **Polarity `applies-light`.** It puts `light` on its own root and brings its own ground. You do NOT owe it the light class, and adding one around it is redundant at best.
- **`chrome.*` is invariant.** This module is in the fixed-dark world, so the distinction bites here: `chrome.*` never inverts and belongs only on a surface that never flips. Anything on a surface that follows the theme uses `text.*` / `surface.*`. The two share their dark values, so the wrong pick looks correct until someone opens light mode.
- **Content is props.** This section hardcodes no copy; the page supplies it.

These are the rules this module can break. The full set, the surface roles and
